# Top Gun Surgeon — Roadmap

## Purpose
**Get kids interested in surgery, and teach them the rules of the Top Gun laparoscopic drills.**
Players are kids, roughly ages 9–14, mostly on tablets, phones and school Chromebooks, often with a parent or teacher nearby. Every decision below is judged against two questions: *does this make a kid want to keep playing?* and *does this teach them a drill rule?*

Only one thing is fixed: **the fundamental rules of each drill** (what you transfer, where, in what order, with which hand, what counts as a mistake, how many reps). Everything else was rethought.

---

## The rethink

### 1. What the game is
**Now:** a menu of five drills; the "Top Gun" theme is only a logo.
**Instead: flight school for future surgeons.** You're a cadet earning your wings. Each play is a short **mission** (sortie) with a briefing and a debrief; you climb pilot ranks; your instruments live in a hangar. The theme is the structure, and it's fun for kids without being childish.

### 2. Teaching the rules (the core purpose)
**Now:** a paragraph of text before each drill. Kids skip it, then fail without knowing why.
**Instead:**
- **Rule school for every drill**: a short guided first mission where each rule is taught by doing it once — "Grab the **red** part" (the game waits until you do), "Pass it across the **orange line**", "Drop it on the **blue** peg". Rules unlock one at a time; no reading walls.
- **Rule cards**: each rule is one picture + one short sentence, collected in a "Rule book" you can flip through anytime.
- **Mistakes teach**: when a rule is broken, the game pauses for a beat, shows *which* rule with its card ("Rule 2: only grab red!"), and lets you try again. No silent penalties.
- **Quick rule quiz** after a drill's rule school ("Which part can you grab?") to check understanding — a star for each right answer.

### 3. Inspiration: why surgery is cool
**Now:** nothing connects the game to real surgery.
**Instead:** short "**Real surgeon**" cards between missions — what keyhole surgery is, why surgeons practise these exact drills, how a camera inside the body works, fun facts ("Surgeons can tie knots inside a body through holes smaller than a pencil"). Unlockable short clips/photos of real training boxes and surgical robots. Rank-ups say what that level of surgeon does in real life. Optional "Ask a surgeon" content for classrooms.

### 4. Controls (kid-first)
**Now:** joysticks/keys set *speed*, so the tip overshoots; grip is a toggle; two instruments need two hands on one keyboard.
**Instead: drag to move, hold to grip — one model on every device.**
- **Touch (main platform):** drag anywhere on the view and the tip follows your finger's movement (relative, so your finger never hides the target). A big **hold-to-grip** button under the other thumb. Height is a chunky slider on the screen edge.
- **Mouse:** the tip goes where you point; **hold the button to grip, let go to release** — natural drag-and-drop, while the instrument still does the work so the drill rules stay intact. Wheel or the on-screen slider sets height.
- **Gamepad and keyboard** as alternatives.
- **Rotation only where it matters** (the hook). Jaws line up automatically elsewhere.
- **Two-instrument drills:** you control the instrument the rules say is next; the other holds still. Kids never juggle two hands on one keyboard.

### 5. Seeing depth (the hardest skill)
Depth aids are training wheels kids graduate off: Beginner gets autopilot height, a strong shadow and the height gauge (the side-view window was removed after play-testing); higher levels remove them one at a time until it's scope-only, like real surgery.

### 6. Physics that feels real
**Now:** nothing collides with the jaws, so a grabbed pea passes through the metal; each drill fakes its own physics.
**Instead:** a real physics engine (Rapier). Instruments are solid; jaws close until they touch the object and hold it where they caught it; objects roll, slide, swing and stack believably. Real behaviour teaches real rules: a pea really does roll off the dome if you drop it on the edge.

### 7. Feedback, scoring and tone
**Now:** a 6-part formula, "errors", "strikes", "dimensionless jerk".
**Instead:**
- **Three stars per mission**: finish it, finish it with few mistakes, finish it fast and smoothly. Stars are simple and every kid knows them.
- Friendly words: "bumps" not errors, "Accuracy / Speed / Smooth hands" not precision/control.
- **Encouraging debrief**: one highlight ("Your fastest grab yet!") and one tip in plain language ("Lift up before you move — then you won't bump the plate").
- The detailed numbers stay available in a "For grown-ups" panel for teachers and parents.

### 8. Levels and progression
**Now:** an opaque "score 40 to unlock".
**Instead:** earn **1 star to unlock the next level**. Each level card shows what changes with an icon ("the cup moves!"). Pilot ranks from XP; a free reward track (instrument skins, plane decals, HUD colours) so something unlocks every few missions. Coins are earned at a kid-friendly pace and can **never** be bought with real money.

### 9. Session shape
Missions under 2 minutes; instant retry; a daily "flight plan" of 3 missions; no timers that punish stepping away (auto-pause).

### 10. Safety, privacy and classrooms
- **No accounts, no personal data, no public names.** Callsigns are picked from fun word lists (or typed, filtered) and stay on the device.
- **No open social features.** Sharing happens through **classrooms**: a teacher creates a class code; the class gets its own leaderboard and progress view. Nothing is visible to strangers.
- No ads, no purchases, no external links without a grown-up gate. Designed to fit children's-privacy rules (COPPA / GDPR-K) before any online feature ships.
- Gentle realism: the suturing pad looks like a practice pad, not a wound.

### 11. Look and sound
**Colour review (kids lens):** the original near-black + orange with condensed military fonts reads as a gritty night-time fighter cockpit — adult, a bit intimidating, and hard to read for younger players.
**Instead: "golden-hour sky".** Deep sky-navy backgrounds (not black), your orange kept as the brand/action colour, with sky blue, mint green and sunshine yellow as friendly accents and pink reserved for celebrations. Rounded, bold, highly readable fonts (Fredoka for headings, Nunito for text), less letter-spacing, bigger sizes. The 3D world stays real and calm so the drills still feel like real surgery training. Sound tells you what you touched; music builds as you do well; short voice callouts ("Nice grab!", "Three to go!") help younger kids and non-readers.

### 11b. Keeping kids coming back (healthy engagement)
The goal is repetition: kids play the drills again and again until the rules and movements are second nature, and come away curious about surgery. What hooks kids, done ethically:
- **A buddy character.** A friendly pilot-surgeon mascot who briefs missions, cheers good grabs, explains a broken rule, and shares surgery facts. Kids return to characters, not menus.
- **A campaign map.** Missions laid out across an island of airbases; each base is a drill level with 3 stars to earn; new bases open as you go. Clear "next thing" every session.
- **Visible mastery.** Stars, personal-best times, a ghost of your best run to race, "fastest grab yet!" callouts. Beating yourself is the most repeatable motivation.
- **Collections.** Instrument skins, plane stickers, pilot patches and an album of real-surgery fact cards that fill up as you play.
- **Predictable rewards with small surprises.** A reward track that pays every few missions, plus an occasional bonus sticker after a great run. **No loot boxes, nothing random tied to payment, nothing for sale.**
- **Daily flight plan and streaks — gentle.** Three short missions a day; streaks have free "freeze" days and never punish a missed day.
- **Variants that keep the rules.** Same drill rules, new twists: night mode (darker scope), "mirror world", speed challenge, pea rain, class-wide weekly challenge.
- **Short sessions, big feelings.** Missions under two minutes, instant replay, juicy feedback.
- **Healthy limits.** A friendly "great flying — time for a break?" after long sessions; parent/teacher time settings; no notifications to kids.
- **The surgery hook.** Every rank-up unlocks a "real surgeon" story: what that drill trains for in real operations, a robot or camera fact, a short clip.

### 12. Accessibility
Shape + colour for every signal, big touch targets, adjustable text size, reduced motion, one-handed mode, left-handed layouts, voice-over for instructions.

### 13. Technology
Stay a static, no-build site for now (Rapier loads from a CDN); revisit Vite + TypeScript when online classrooms arrive. Installable as an app, works offline.

### 14. Name check
"Top Gun" is a Paramount trademark — confirm the name before publishing publicly.

### Phase 3 — Identity, structure and UI ✅
Home with one-tap Fly, tab bar pages, briefing sheet, pause menu, debrief, reward moments, PWA. Re-skinned in the kid-friendly "golden-hour sky" palette and rounded fonts during Phase 4.

### Phase 4 — Controls and physics rebuild ✅
**Done**
- Drag-to-move + hold-to-grip on every device: mouse (tip follows the pointer, hold = grip, wheel = height, right-click/Tab swaps hands), touch (trackpad drag, big hold-to-grip pad, height slider; split-screen drag + tap-to-grip for two-hand drills), gamepad, keyboard. Height is now straight up/down.
- The pointer drives the instrument the rules say is next (Cylinder automatically; Cobra follows the expected hand, swappable). An instrument that stops being active keeps its grip, so one-pointer hand-offs work.
- Rapier physics engine (fixed 120 Hz) with solid jaws and contact grasping. **Pea Drop runs on it:** jaws stop on the bean's surface, the bean is held exactly where it was caught, peas roll off the dome's shoulder for real, and a sensor in the throat counts the drop.
- Cobra Rope no longer passes through the instruments.
- Beginner side-view window (depth aid) and auto-framing camera for every screen shape.

- **Cylinder Transfer on physics:** solid hollow winged cylinders, solid pegs and board; a cylinder only leaves its peg when lifted straight up; released over the right peg it lines up and slides down by gravity; grabbing a cylinder the rules don't allow is refused and flagged.
- **Triangle Transfer on physics:** a solid wire hook and a solid triangle with a real ring. The catch uses a light assist (a pivot joint when the hook's bend is inside the ring and rising) so it is reliable for kids; swinging, landing, and slipping off when the hook is tipped or whipped are all physical.
- Kid-friendly re-skin: "airshow sticker / flight logbook" style, hand-drawn two-tone icon set, Bungee + Atkinson Hyperlegible type.

- **Suturing needle driving:** once the tip is in the tissue the needle can't be shoved sideways ("Don't push — roll your wrist!"); rolling the wrist (Q/E, or dragging sideways) drives it along its own curve and out the other side of the incision for a bite. A needle-loading helper lines the curve up across the cut when the tip first enters.
- **Training wheels by level:** Beginner has the side-view window and grab helper; Intermediate keeps the grab helper; Advanced and Expert have no helpers. Each level card and briefing says which helpers are on.

### Phase 5 — Teach the rules, with a buddy ✅
- **Difficulty pass** (play-tested with a "kid" bot, `tools/kidbot.js`, and checked against the Top Gun eBook): every drill is now finishable at Beginner (Pea ~135 s, Triangle ~98 s, Cobra ~185 s, Cylinder ~220 s) and the levels get harder step by step. Fixes: Cylinder hand-off stall; Cobra rope pick-up forgiveness by level; crossing turned into a soft wall below Expert, since the flat camera view can't show the depth the real rope runs along; an empty idle hand steps aside for the active one; Triangle hook tolerance ladder; no penalties after a drill is finished.
- **Scout**, a hand-drawn paper-plane buddy with moods, who can read lines aloud (Settings → "Scout reads aloud").
- **Rule school** for Pea, Triangle, Cobra and Cylinder: a guided Beginner lesson where each rule is taught by doing it, then a 3-question **quiz** with a star per right answer.
- **Rule cards** (picture + one sentence + the official Top Gun penalty) for 20 rules, collected in a **Rule book** (Home, Drills → Rules).
- **Mistakes teach**: a broken rule shows its card ("Oops! Rule 3: No dragging") with a slow-motion beat on Beginner.
- **Three stars** per mission (finish · few bumps · 75+ points); stars replace medals everywhere, and **1 star unlocks** the next level.
- Friendly words: bumps, Accuracy / Speed / Smooth hands, a highlight and a plain tip from Scout, "For grown-ups" for the detailed numbers.
- **Real surgeon** fact cards on Home and in every debrief.
- Moved to Phase 8: the kid-friendly suturing look.

### Phase 6 — The hook: campaign and collections ✅
- **Campaign map** (Map tab): four islands, one per level (Beginner Bay → Top Gun Peak), 20 bases: the four drills per island plus a variant base. Each base has 3 stars; the next opens once the one before has a star. Scout waits at your current base; Home's hero always shows it.
- **Variants that keep the rules**: Night flight (lights out, a scope light follows your instrument) and Speed run (third star = beat par).
- **Ghost racing**: your fastest finished run replays as a see-through marker; the HUD shows your best, and every rep says how far ahead or behind you are ("−1.0 s vs your best"). Beating it earns a sticker.
- **Album** tab: 24 hand-made stickers (achievements, reward-track wings, a few free surprises after 3-star runs), a patch per drill (bronze: finish all levels, gold: 3 stars on all), and the Real surgeon card collection. Skins live behind the Album's Skins button.
- **Reward track**: a reward every 3 missions, in order (coins and wings stickers).
- **Daily flight plan**: three short missions a day (+150 coins), building a "trail" streak with free freeze days (2 to start, +1 a week, max 3); a missed day never shames, it just starts a new trail.
- **Healthy breaks**: after ~20 minutes of flying, Scout suggests a stretch.
- Nothing can be bought, no loot boxes; the surprise stickers are free and only cosmetic.
- Not done (ideas for later): mirror world and pea rain variants, rank-up "real surgeon" stories, parent/teacher time settings (Phase 7).

### Phase 7 — Classrooms (safe online) — skipped for now
Teacher class codes, class leaderboards and progress view, privacy review (COPPA/GDPR-K). Not needed for the first release; the game stays fully offline with no accounts.

### Phase 8 — Polish and ship ✅ (publishing waits on the name check)
- **Kid-friendly suturing look**: a mint silicone practice pad with printed stitch-guide dots and a white foam core — no wound colours.
- **Adaptive music**, synthesized live (no downloads): calm in menus; in drills it builds layer by layer (bass, arpeggio, drums and a little hook) with your clean streak and drops back after a bump. Follows the Music setting, pauses when hidden.
- **Performance on school devices**: smaller shadows on low-memory/low-core devices; an automatic smooth mode steps quality down (shadows → lens effects → resolution → no shadows) if a drill stays under ~45 fps (Settings → Auto smooth mode).
- **Accessibility**: Text size (Normal / Large / Huge), reduce motion follows the device by default, screen-reader announcements for callouts, Scout and rule cards, thick keyboard focus rings, shape + colour signals, Scout reads aloud.
- **Offline and install**: the service worker precaches the whole game including three.js and Rapier (`node tools/precache.mjs` before each deploy); install colours and orientation fixed.
- README rewritten with run and deploy steps.
- Home layout: the hero stays in view beside the taller side column.
- **Easier controls (after play-testing)**: assists as training wheels that keep every rule — Beginner/Rule school: *Autopilot height* (point, press GRAB, it dives and grabs; drop targets pull you into line; steady speed; Triangle threads and sets down the hook for you); Intermediate: *Height magnet* + miss hints; Advanced: miss hints only; Expert: none. Tap-to-grip by default (Settings). W goes in, S comes out. Lessons start with a "move around" step. Assisted kid bot at Beginner: Pea 100 s / 0 bumps, Triangle 83–106 s / 0–1, Cylinder 158 s / 0 (was 135 s, 98 s, 220 s with misses).
- **Still open**: play-testing with real kids; the name check ("Top Gun" is a Paramount trademark and the name of the Top Gun Laparoscopic Skills program); publishing on the veridien website (needs your go-ahead).
